## Findings

### 1. Systemic CHANGELOG gap — 12 landed tasks undocumented (fixed)

**Severity: moderate.** Every task in this range landed with **zero** CHANGELOG
entries. The last documented tasks were 333 and 352; tasks 332, 335, 336, 337,
338, 353, 357, 365, 367, 368, 369, and 370 had none. The project's own convention
("a task isn't complete until affected docs are updated: `roadmap/tasks.toml`,
`CHANGELOG.md`, …") was silently unenforced across twelve consecutive deliveries.

**Root cause — this is a workflow hole, not twelve individual oversights.**
Task 367 deliberately excluded `CHANGELOG.md` from delivery commits (so a reviewer
filing from a stale fork can't collide on it) and explicitly listed "CHANGELOG
generation from verdicts" as `out_of_scope`. That left the orchestrator as the
implicit owner — and in practice no step executes it. Note the gap predates 367's
exclusion (332–357 landed before it shipped), so the exclusion widened an existing
hole rather than creating it.

**Fixed forward:** wrote all twelve entries in house style, placed by kind —
- `### Added`: 368 (coalesce-dispatch), 367 (`proposed_tasks` proposals), 369
  (lander additive-conflict renumber), 370 (settle-persist dead-letter + replayer),
  332 (dep-bump dispatch), 335 (tooling-baseline dispatch), 336 (Rust freshness provider).
- `### Changed`: 353 (`Run` gen_statem decomposition), 357 (`Run.Actions`
  decomposition), 337+338 (Reach smell triage, merged into one entry — they are two
  halves of one cleanup and read as one change).
- `### Fixed`: 365 (Postgres tolerant row decode).

Entries were written from each task's acceptance criteria plus the landed code, not
from the boilerplate `implemented` notes (which only record "landed by X via
merge-train" and carry no substance).

**Filed as [task 371](roadmap/tasks.toml)** — *"Close the post-land CHANGELOG gap:
no workflow step owns writing entries."* The durable fix is assigning ownership to
exactly one step and making the omission mechanically detectable; hand-backfilling
in an audit is the symptom, not the cure. The task carries the mantra constraint
explicitly: harness may *detect the absence* of an entry, but must not classify
entries into Added/Changed/Fixed by keyword regex — that's judgment, and it belongs
to an agent.
